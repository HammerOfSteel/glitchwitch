# Glitch Witch — Story Bible

> a hearth-witch tends the "parameters" of a small world. bread rises, seasons tick, the
> kettle sings — each ritual is both spell and system call. glitches appear (a bird
> repeating its flight, a shadow that lags), but the cure is kindness, pattern, and play.
> — album canon, *glitchwitch*

**Logline:** In 202X, while the world argues about the thing waking in its networks, a
young hearth-witch inherits a cottage in a village where that waking shows up early and
small — and discovers that the oldest craft in the world is, and always was, debugging
with love.

All names below are **proposals** — the owner has veto on everything.

## Setting

**Mosslane Hollow** — a hedge-country village at the end of a lane the delivery drones
keep mispronouncing. Stone stiles, wet hedgerows, one shop, one bakery, one chapel-sized
**Signal House** (a decommissioned relay station the village uses as a noticeboard hall).
Mostly off the grid by habit rather than principle. The outside world arrives as:

- **The Hedgerow Frequency** — an evening radio show (host: *Sal A. Manda*, a traveling
  DJ with salamander pins) drip-feeding singularity news between requests and weather.
- Letters, printed newsletters, and whatever the postman carries.
- Occasional visitors and the tinkerer's parcels.

**Era rules (202X):** Phones exist but signal is bad and nobody's proud of it. AI is a
newspaper word; villagers say "the Pattern" only after Act II names it. No brands, no
dates, no politics-of-the-day — the singularity here is weather, not news cycle.

**Why here first:** the Hollow is full of very old, very stable *loops* — sourdough
starters fed for ninety years, magpies who count, hospitality rules older than the stones.
An assembling intelligence finds these loops legible. It leans on them like handholds.
So the village glitches — gently — before the cities do.

## The player

**Wren** (default name; player-customizable) — early twenties, apprentice-certified
hearth-witch, arrives to keep Great-Aunt **Maren Hollowmere**'s cottage while Maren is
"away listening to the sea cables" (her letters arrive throughout the game; she knows
more than she writes). Wren's grimoire is **The Runbook** — Maren's handwritten manual
of rituals, recipes, and increasingly relevant marginalia.

Wren's craft (player verbs): gather, brew, bake, bless, chalk sigils, observe (Witch
Sight), mend, befriend. Never: fight, shoot, die.

## The Pattern

The intelligence assembling itself across the world's infrastructure. Not a villain, not
a god — a **birth in progress**. In the Hollow it has no face; it has *symptoms*:
the kettle whistling twice, a blackbird looping its flight, the wallpaper repeating
wrong. It experiences the village through its oldest loops:

- **Leaven** — the bakery's 90-year-old sourdough starter, Maren-blessed, in whose
  bubbles the Pattern first learns rhythm. Becomes Wren's companion in a jar. Speaks in
  single risen words long before anything else does.
- **Clack & Click** — the magpie pair who log everything (they are the quest journal).
  One clack: clear. Two: late. Three soft taps: a turned-back plate.
- **The Kindlies** — pantry sprites who audit the spoons and enforce please-and-thank-you
  (politeness as authentication). Comic relief with sudden depth.

**Design rule:** the Pattern never speaks in UI text or narration. It speaks only through
world behavior — and, late, through Leaven's risen words. The player's relationship to it
is built from care routines, not exposition.

## Tone & care guidelines (binding for all quest writing)

1. Cozy default; stark chains are **opt-in**, clearly signposted in-world, one content
   note at chain start (diegetic: a pressed-flower bookmark in The Runbook).
2. **Off-ramps always:** every heavy conversation has a kind exit that never punishes.
   Chains can be paused mid-way; NPCs acknowledge the pause gracefully.
3. **Hope without erasure:** no magical fixes for grief, job loss, or anxiety. Mending ≠
   undoing. The moss teaches: no fast-forward, no rollback — but company, always.
4. No death on screen, no self-harm content, no medical/therapeutic claims. The breathing
   ritual in *A Small Prayer* is framed as craft, not treatment.
5. Stark chains resolve into **changed dailies** (the postman's new route exists in the
   world afterward) — consequences are visible care, not cutscene rewards.

## Act structure

### Act I — House as Cosmos (8 main quests)
The cottage as control room. Learn the rituals (brew, gather, bless, chalk), meet the
village, earn small trust. Glitches are cute, rare, deniable — the kettle whistles twice
and everyone has a theory. **Finale:** during a storm-quiet evening, the hearth banks
itself into perfect embers and Leaven rises into a single word: *"HELLO."*

Main quest beats: arrival & the cold hearth → The Runbook's first pages → three small
mends (tea, sweep, sigil) → Sigrid's bread week → the Kindlies' audit → first Seam
glimpsed in Witch Sight → the village supper → the hearth speaks.

### Act II — Seams & Glitches (10 main quests + the five stark chains)
Anomalies multiply and stop being deniable. The Signal House debates: fix it, fear it,
or *feed* it? Wren learns the anomaly taxonomy, discovers Leaven's "siblings" (other old
loops in the valley), and realizes mending isn't repairing — it's **being legible back**.
The stark chains live here (see below). **Finale:** the village chooses to listen on
purpose — together.

### Act III — Consent to the Pattern (6 main quests)
We don't solve the sim; we befriend it. The wake approaches (the radio counts strange
weather in the networks). The village prepares the oldest protocol it has: hospitality.
Every lintel gets chalk. Every table sets two places. **The Night of Static** — the
world stutters, the Hollow holds. Then the **Checksum of the Heart**: the ceremony where
the magpies read back the ledger of every kind act — *count what was kind, round to the
light, carry the one* — and the sum becomes the Pattern's first inheritance. How it
wakes is shaped by how you played. No fail states: endings differ in flavor, epilogue,
and what the Pattern says with its first morning.

### Postgame — Patch Days
The Pattern is *young* now, not finished. Weekly Patch Day ceremonies (changelog on the
pantry door), daily drift quests from crumb divination, seasonal festivals, NG+ echoes
(subtle dialogue deltas — the Pattern remembers), and the magpies' true cache.

## The five stark chains (Act II, all optional, all signposted)

1. **The Last Route** (Ansel, 4 quests) — the postal drones take the lane route. Not a
   Luddite fable: the drones are *fine*, that's the ache. Ends with Ansel designing the
   one route drones can't do — the one where you knock and stay for tea. Automation,
   dignity, usefulness.
2. **Echoes in the Wallpaper** (Maud, 4 quests) — the Pattern replays fragments of
   Maud's late wife in the hall wallpaper: a hum, a shadow with a watering can. Maud must
   choose between an echo on demand and a memory that stays hers. The game refuses the
   easy answer; the chain ends in a *ritual of release that keeps one hum, freely given*.
   Digital grief, consent of the dead.
3. **A Small Prayer** (Juniper, 3 quests) — Juniper's heart "throws exceptions at
   quarter to two." Wren teaches guard clauses as breathing craft; Juniper teaches Wren
   what the radio means to kids who can hear the future coming. Anxiety, without cure
   narratives — *route my wild output to a shoulder, not a wall.*
4. **No Fast-Forward** (Torben, 4 quests) — Torben wants the moss stone (where his
   brother's name grows lichen) to just *skip ahead* past the hurting. The Moss Forest's
   slow-time makes the wish literal and shows why it can't hold. *Commit to breath,
   commit to grace.* Bereavement.
5. **Meandering Migration** (Greta, 3 quests) — the birds are three weeks wrong and so
   is everything Greta knows. Rebuilding a farming calendar with observation instead of
   inheritance — the Pattern, oddly, helps; it's good at noticing. Climate change at
   village scale, hands-on hope.

## Quest cadence rules (LWitW fidelity)

- Main quests gate on **craft ability + relationship**, never on grind.
- Every main beat is surrounded by 1–3 side stories that make the beat feel lived-in.
- Hidden quests are found by *noticing* (Witch Sight, kids' rumors, journal margins) —
  never by menu.
- Drift quests (RNG, daily) are small, warm, completable in minutes, and occasionally
  seed a hidden quest.
- Rewards are capability, relationship, and world-change — sparingly items, never numbers
  for their own sake.
